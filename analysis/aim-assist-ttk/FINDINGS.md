# Aim assist and gun time-to-kill audit

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Scope:** Compare CSVM's player gun aim assist, gun cadence, aircraft damage, and hit reactions with the decoded retail executable and shipped data. No gameplay code was changed.

## Verdict

The aim-assist implementation itself is a close port of the original. Its important constants, candidate gates, intercept calculation, smoothing, forget timer, and 1-degree shot error all match the executable. The focused aim-assist and air-to-air suites pass. There is no evidence that CSVM weakened the assist or halved the gun rate.

The longer effective kill time is credible, but the strongest causes are outside `AimAssist`:

1. **Confirmed, broad:** CSVM does not apply the original enemy difficulty multiplier to armour and health. Original Novice enemies have `0.875x` pools; CSVM uses approximately Veteran (`1.0x`) pools. That alone makes a CSVM enemy take about **14.3% more damage** to kill than the same original Novice enemy.
2. **Confirmed, campaign-specific and potentially severe:** CSVM parses but drops roster `init_health` and `armor` overrides. Eighteen enabled enemy roster entries explicitly set `armor = 0` in the original. CSVM gives those aircraft their full default armour, making representative gun-hit counts almost **2x** the original.
3. **Confirmed behavioural invention, observed at runtime:** after a failed steady-hand roll, CSVM immediately starts a randomly selected evasive maneuver. The latest executable decode says the original sets an evade **flag**, not the evasive-maneuver mode; the flag suppresses lay-off/callouts and is cleared by pursuer alignment. In the probe, CSVM entered `scissors` after the first 40-calibre hit and left the 6-degree assist cone. This can substantially reduce hit rate and increase real elapsed TTK.
4. **Unverified fidelity:** CSVM aircraft projectile hitboxes are generated by tuned convex-decomposition thresholds. The original aircraft hit geometry has not been matched. This may change hit rate, but the available evidence does not establish the direction.

Do **not** retune sticky aim-assist constants to address this symptom. Fix or isolate durability seeding and the hit-reaction divergence first.

## What the original aim assist does

The executable decode in [`docs/org/aim-assist.md`](../../docs/org/aim-assist.md) establishes this pipeline:

- It changes the launch direction at the instant of firing; it does not steer bullets after launch.
- It operates only for the local human player.
- State is held per gun muzzle: smoothed direction, selected target direction, and last update.
- It scans hostile live vehicles, turrets, mission structures, and eligible in-flight ordnance.
- It solves a constant-velocity intercept using shooter-relative target velocity.
- It rejects candidates outside weapon range or the gun's `CANNON_SPREAD` half-cone. Every shipped gun uses 6 degrees.
- `CANNON_SPREAD` is an aim-assist acceptance cone, **not** random gun dispersion.
- Candidate score is alignment minus distance times `dist_factor`. Shipped `dist_factor` is zero, so the best-aligned target wins.
- The target direction is approached at `catchup_rate = 5/s`, approximately 0.2 seconds, and forgotten 1.5 seconds after the last shot.
- The only shipped random error is `sticky_bullet_inaccuracy = 1 degree`, sampled with a uniformly distributed polar angle.
- The HUD pipper remains on the nose-axis ballistic solution. It intentionally does not reveal the assisted launch vector.

## Gun cadence and theoretical TTK

The shipped `wep_40` data is:

- armour damage: 4.5
- health damage: 4.5
- fire rate: 8 rounds/s
- velocity: 900 m/s
- range: 1000 m
- assist cone: 6 degrees

The original selects one gun group, emits one round per fire tick, and alternates its muzzles. Thus a two-gun 40-calibre group still emits **8 rounds/s total**, not 16. CSVM reproduces this. The earlier apparent 12–13 rounds/s reading from footage was a redraw artifact; [`docs/org/weaponFire.md`](../../docs/org/weaponFire.md) records the executable result.

A base Fury authors 72 armour and 72 health. With every round hitting and no jitter, `wep_40` therefore needs 32 hits and kills in about **3.875 seconds** from an immediate first shot. The original difficulty scale changes that baseline as follows:

| Original difficulty | Fury armour + health | `wep_40` hits | Ideal burst time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Novice (`0.875x`) | 63 + 63 | 28 | 3.375 s |
| Veteran (`1.0x`) | 72 + 72 | 32 | 3.875 s |
| Ace (`1.25x`) | 90 + 90 | 40 | 4.875 s |

Aircraft also receive the decoded independent +/-5% per-spawn variation. Actual elapsed TTK is then dominated by hit rate: range, aspect, whether the target remains inside the 6-degree cone, and collision geometry.

## Confirmed durability omissions

### Enemy difficulty does not scale damage pools

The original roster spawn multiplies enemy armour and health by `0.875`, `1.0`, or `1.25`. In Instant Action, the Novice/Veteran/Ace wave skill is specifically this pool multiplier; it is not the pilot rating.

CSVM exposes and stores `InstantActionWave.EnemySkill`, but the spawn record carries no difficulty or health-scale field. [`InstantActionDirector.cs`](../../CSVM/src/Session/InstantActionDirector.cs) chooses pilot statistics, while [`AiFlightAssembler.cs`](../../CSVM/src/Session/AiFlightAssembler.cs) applies the faithful +/-5% aircraft jitter to the unscaled airframe pools. The selected wave skill therefore does not change enemy durability.

This explains a longer CSVM kill time when the comparison is against original Novice. Against original Veteran, this omission does not change the base pool; against original Ace, CSVM is easier.

### Campaign roster health and armour overrides are dropped

The original applies roster slot 7 `init_health` when greater than zero and slot 66 `armor` when greater than or equal to zero, then applies difficulty. The format and executable path are documented in [`docs/formats/ai-rosters.md`](../../docs/formats/ai-rosters.md) and [`docs/org/vehicleDamage.md`](../../docs/org/vehicleDamage.md).

CSVM's [`RosterSpawnPlan`](../../CSVM/src/Session/CampaignRoster.cs) has neither value. `CampaignRosterPlan.Build` does not read them, `SpawnFor` cannot forward them, and the assembler seeds the airframe defaults instead.

A census of all 414 extracted roster blocks found, among 251 enabled non-player-team blocks:

- 25 with a positive `init_health` override;
- 38 with a non-negative `armor` override;
- 18 hostile entries with `armor = 0`.

Those 18 are five Peacemakers in C2/M05, ten Bloodhawks/Kestrels in C2B/M04, and three Kestrels in C3/M01. Ignoring the zero is directly in the direction reported by the player:

| Example, Veteran, no jitter | Original roster result | Current CSVM result | 40-calibre hits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Peacemaker (`armor 0`) | 0 + 68 | 68 + 68 | 16 vs 31 |
| Bloodhawk (`armor 0`) | 0 + 64 | 64 + 64 | 15 vs 29 |
| Kestrel (`armor 0`) | 0 + 84 | 84 + 84 | 19 vs 38 |

Positive roster overrides can have the opposite effect: dropping an authored boss health/armour increase can make CSVM easier. The omission is therefore mission-dependent, but the zero-armour cases are nearly a 2x gun-TTK error.

## Confirmed hit-reaction invention

The most relevant control-flow mismatch is in [`AiModeMachine.NotifyDamage`](../../CSVM/src/Flight/AiModeMachine.cs):

- CSVM rolls `steady_hand_chance` after a hit.
- On failure it calls `PickManeuver()` and transitions directly to `EvasiveManeuver`; if none is eligible, it uses an explicitly invented eight-second plain evade with random 60–120-degree heading scrambles.
- The latest executable decode in [`docs/org/aiControlLaw.md`](../../docs/org/aiControlLaw.md) says failure sets vehicle byte `+0xBA`, an evade flag. The flag suppresses lay-off and voice callouts and is cleared when pursuer nose alignment drops below 0.85. It is distinct from the mode field and does not itself enter the evasive-maneuver executor.

That distinction appeared in the runtime probe. The first 40-calibre hit failed the roll and CSVM immediately selected shipped maneuver `scissors`. The target crossed out of the player's 6-degree assist cone; only two rounds hit during the 20-second non-tracking scripted run. The probe is not a human-play TTK measurement, because the scripted player does not turn after the target, but it demonstrates the mechanism that can turn matched damage into a much longer effective kill.

The absorbed damage amount is logged but does not affect CSVM's roll. A design-level damage weighting is mentioned in code as undecoded; it cannot yet be used as original-ground-truth evidence.

## Other unverified or minor divergences

- [`PlaneCollider.cs`](../../CSVM/src/Flight/PlaneCollider.cs) creates aircraft hitboxes from model triangles using several constants explicitly marked `TUNE` (wing band, tail split, minimum thickness, volume split, and part limits). These are the projectile hit volumes. Original geometric hit resolution is still untraced, so hit-rate parity is not established.
- The exact-square-root intercept and Godot interpolation safeguards are CSVM implementation differences, but both improve numerical stability and are very unlikely to increase TTK.
- CSVM extends assist to multiple local human panes. This is intentional split-screen support and cannot explain weaker guns.
- Ground/structure candidate population is an approximation. That can change which object wins when several ground targets overlap the cone, but it does not explain a lone aircraft taking more gunfire.

## What is not missing

- There is no second simultaneous bullet from a two-muzzle gun group in the original. CSVM's alternating-muzzle 8 rounds/s cadence is correct.
- `CANNON_SPREAD` must not be added as random dispersion. It is the 6-degree acquisition half-cone.
- The 1-degree sticky inaccuracy is already present and uses the original's unusual polar-uniform distribution.
- Armour-first damage with 1:1 overflow, whole-health death at zero, and the projectile-to-aircraft damage route are present and pass the focused air-to-air suite.
- The visual pipper not following the assisted vector is original behaviour, not evidence that the assist missed.

## Remaining evidence gap

The repository has original gun-firing footage but no matched original kill capture with known aircraft, gun group, difficulty, range, and target behaviour. Consequently this report can prove code/data mismatches and compute ideal hit-count TTK, but it cannot yet give an empirical original-versus-CSVM elapsed-time ratio for ordinary play.

The decisive follow-up capture would use the same player aircraft and gun group against the same enemy airframe at a known difficulty, recording trigger-down-to-kill plus rounds fired/hits. A Veteran pass isolates hit reaction and collision geometry; a Novice pass additionally verifies the missing `0.875x` pool scale. Until that exists, the evidence ranking is:

1. roster pool overrides: confirmed and sometimes almost 2x;
2. difficulty pool scale: confirmed and 14.3% against original Novice;
3. damage-triggered maneuver transition: confirmed CSVM invention and observed hit-rate loss;
4. collision geometry: plausible but direction unmeasured;
5. sticky aim-assist strength: low-probability cause; executable comparison and tests agree.

## Correction: no shipped hostile authors `armor 0`

A re-run of the roster census against the extracted data
([`Census-RosterDurability.ps1`](Census-RosterDurability.ps1)) reproduces this report's structural
numbers exactly (414 blocks, 251 enabled non-player-team, 25 with a positive `init_health`) and
overturns the `armor 0` finding above.

**Slot 66 does not exist on those 18 blocks.** Roster blocks are not fixed-width: the field-count
histogram is `{42:20, 65:1, 66:33, 67:14, 68:39, 81:307}`, and a block of 66 fields carries indices
0 to 65 only. The five Peacemakers in C2/M05, the ten Bloodhawks and Kestrels in C2B/M04 and the
three Kestrels in C3/M01 are all 66-field blocks, which is why they add to exactly 18. They author
no armour override, the airframe default applies, and CSVM's behaviour on them is already correct.
The same off-by-one inflates the armour-override count: 20 blocks author a non-negative `armor`, not
38, and the 18 absent slots make up the difference.

Every real override RAISES durability. The 20 values run 90, 96 (x2), 102 (x2), 108 (x7), 114, 126
(x6) and 132, and each sits on a mission's named ace. Dropping them therefore makes CSVM's aces
**softer** than the original, not tougher, so the evidence ranking above inverts on this point:
fixing `BL-557` lengthens time to kill on the hard fights rather than shortening it, and it cannot
be the cause of a slow kill felt in ordinary play.

The reading rule this cost, which [`docs/formats/ai-rosters.md`](../../docs/formats/ai-rosters.md)
already states in bold: **treat a missing slot as unset, never as zero.** A census that maps absent
to `0.0` invents authored data, and here it invented an entire class of armour-stripped enemy.

## The difficulty scale is the campaign-wide one, not a Novice-only case

The ranking above qualifies the missing `0.875` multiplier as costing 14.3 % "against original
Novice", which reads as a corner case. It is not one. The campaign's difficulty selector has its own
vocabulary: `rof/ui_strings.json` ids 109-111 are `IDS_DIFFICULTY` = **Normal / Hard / Hardest**,
under `IDS_GO_DIFFICULTY_DESC` "Select the difficulty level for a solo campaign", where Instant
Action's `IDS_IA_DIFFICULTY` is the separate novice/veteran/ace list. Normal is the lowest of the
three, so it is difficulty 0 and takes the `0.875` scale.

A campaign played on Normal therefore meets enemies with 14.3 % more armour and health in CSVM than
in the original, in every mission. That makes the missing scale the broadest of the confirmed causes
and the one to fix first, ahead of the roster overrides, which push time to kill the other way.
